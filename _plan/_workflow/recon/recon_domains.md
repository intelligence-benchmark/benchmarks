# (a) THE 10 BEST SCHEMA STRESS-TESTS, AND EXACTLY WHAT EACH BREAKS

Validate your YAML schema against these ten **before** curating at scale. If all ten fit without hacks, the schema will survive the other 1,500.

1. **RoboArena** (robotics, robo-arena.github.io) — **Breaks:** `tasks` (there is no task list; evaluators choose freely), `metric` (double-blind pairwise A/B preference, not an absolute score), `maintainer` (a distributed network of labs), `reproducibility` (physical DROID robots in different rooms), and `as_of` (a live ranking running through Dec 2026). Forces you to model **pairwise/relative results** and **physical evaluation venues**.
2. **CACHE Challenges** (chemistry, cache-challenge.org) — **Breaks:** `metric` (experimental hit rate from a real binding assay, plus a medicinal-chemistry panel's qualitative commentary), `access` (you must *buy compounds from Enamine* to participate), `cycle` (multi-year, 8 rounds and counting, two phases each), and `reproducible: true|false` (structurally irreproducible). Forces a **wet-lab-validated** result class and a **participant-cost** field.
3. **CASP17** (biology, predictioncenter.org) — **Breaks:** `version` (editions, not versions), `leaderboard` (the output is a set of peer-reviewed assessment papers), `metric` (GDT_TS/lDDT/TM-score/DockQ/ICS/IPS, different per category), and `evaluation_conditions` (a **human-expert category and an automated-server category are separately ranked**, so the same method appears twice with different numbers). Forces **edition/cycle** modeling and a **human-assistance-allowed** condition field.
4. **WeatherBench 2** (climate, sites.research.google/weatherbench) — **Breaks:** the assumption that a benchmark *has* a ranking at all; its authors explicitly say it is not a single-leaderboard challenge. Also breaks `human_baseline` (meaningless here — the comparator is an operational NWP supercomputer model) and `score` (a grid of variable × pressure level × lead time × metric). Forces a **lead-time / horizon** dimension and a **"no aggregate by design"** flag. This is the strongest external validation of your comparability-key differentiator.
5. **ARC-AGI-3** (general intelligence, arcprize.org) — **Breaks:** `one_result_per_model` (two official leaderboards with **different legality rules**: unmodified API systems vs harness-permitted community entries), `environment` (interactive, agent-driven, not a dataset), `score_range` (frontier models below 1% while humans are at 100%), and `competition` (a $2M prize pool with open-source milestone requirements). Forces **harness-legality** into the comparability key, which is exactly your thesis.
6. **Matbench Discovery** (materials, matbench-discovery.materialsproject.org) — **Breaks:** `metric` (one benchmark = stability F1 + energy MAE + phonons + κ_SRME thermal conductivity + MD stability + diatomic PES), and, critically, **`compliance`**: models are grouped into tiers by *what data they were allowed to train on*. Forces a **training-data eligibility** concept — a comparability key on the *training* side, which most LLM-shaped schemas have no slot for.
7. **Virtual Cell Challenge 2026** (biology, arcinstitute.org) — **Breaks:** `human_baseline` (the score's ceiling is **a real biological replicate experiment**, its floor is the cell-context mean), `metric` (six complementary metrics, each independently normalized), and `training_data` (zero-shot only; there is deliberately no challenge training set this year). Forces a **normalization anchor** field that is not "human".
8. **Kaggle Game Arena** (games, kaggle.com/game-arena) — **Breaks:** `score` (unbounded Elo from all-play-all, 40 games per pair), `stability` (a model's rating changes when a *different* model joins the pool, with no change to the model), and `comparable_across_time` (ratings are only meaningful within one pool snapshot). Forces **rating-pool identity + snapshot date** as part of any stored result, and a UI rule that refuses to compare Elo across snapshots.
9. **ForecastBench / Metaculus FutureEval** (society, forecastbench.org, metaculus.com/futureeval) — **Breaks:** `result_is_final` (questions have no answer at submission; scores resolve months-to-years later and are retroactively updated), and `ground_truth_exists`. Forces a **`resolution_status: pending|partial|resolved`** state and a distinction between "measured on" and "resolved as of" dates. Also gives you real human baselines at two tiers (public crowd and superforecasters).
10. **PaperBench** (agents, github.com/openai/preparedness) — **Breaks:** `metric` (a hierarchical rubric of ~8,316 leaf nodes aggregated into a tree score), `grader` (an LLM judge is part of the measurement apparatus, so the judge model and version must be recorded), and `task_count` (20 papers, thousands of scored sub-criteria). Forces **judge model as a first-class evaluation condition** — which you already flagged, but this is the case that proves it.

**Runners-up worth testing if you have budget:** τ²-bench (**pass^k** reliability metric); BEHAVIOR Challenge (score undefined outside a specific OmniGibson/Isaac-Sim build); AgentDojo (utility × security, two orthogonal axes, no scalar); MLE-bench (medal thresholds derived from real human Kaggle distributions); FAIR Universe HiggsML (metric is confidence-interval coverage, not accuracy); AILuminate (ordinal letter grades relative to the state of the art); A2RL Drone Championship (physical hardware, human opponent, lap time); ReXrank (8 metrics, no published aggregate); BBQ (two-number bias score where zero is optimal); FrontierMath (errata — 42% of problems corrected in June 2026 — makes version pinning mandatory); AudioSet (data distributed as YouTube IDs that rot); DCASE 2026 (one name, 7 tasks, new eval set every year); LifeCLEF (ranking eligibility requires publishing a paper).

**Grounded recommendations for curation targets:**

- **Seed release (proof of breadth): ~300 Tier-1 families, ~20–35 per domain, every domain non-empty.** That is the number that makes "cross-domain breadth" a demonstrated fact rather than a claim, and it is the number a reviewer from any one of these fields can scan and say "yes, you got my field right." Below ~15 per domain a specialist will spot the gaps immediately.
- **12–18 month target: ~1,000–1,500 families.** Beyond that, marginal value drops sharply because you are into papers that were never used by anyone but their authors.
- **Effort model:** a high-quality, fully sourced entry (all schema fields, URLs verified, metric definition read from the paper, access model confirmed) is realistically **30–90 minutes with heavy AI assistance**, and closer to 2–3 hours for the stress-test cases. So 300 entries ≈ **150–450 person-hours**, i.e. roughly one part-time person for 4–8 months, or much less if you accept a two-tier quality model (full curation for Tier 1, a lighter stub with name/URL/domain/status for Tier 2).
- **Where the blind-spot value is concentrated:** robotics, chemistry/materials, biology, climate and physics together are **~120 Tier-1 families**. That is the differentiating core, and it is a *tractable* number. Nobody has laid these on one map with a shared vocabulary — the closest existing artefacts are all single-domain (Epoch AI's 80-benchmark hub is LLM-only; HEP ML Living Review is HEP-only; grand-challenge.org is medical imaging only; Inspect Evals is safety/agent only; Open Problems is single-cell only). **That gap is real and I verified it today.**
- **Coverage-gap findings you can publish on day one, each of which I confirmed is genuinely thin or empty:** phylogenetics has no standing benchmark; education/tutoring has no public leaderboard (only learning-gain RCTs); human-comparison psychometric batteries for AI have no canonical benchmark; retrosynthesis has a canonical *dataset* (USPTO-50k) but **no canonical leaderboard**, which is why 2026 papers report mutually inconsistent top-1 numbers on the same 5,007-reaction test set.
